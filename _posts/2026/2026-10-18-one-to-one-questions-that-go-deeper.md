The easiest one-to-one to run is a status update. It's also the least useful: you can get status from the board. The point of the time is the stuff that never makes it onto a ticket.

Here are seven questions I come back to:

1. **What's taking more energy than it should right now?** Gets at friction (process, tooling, people) without asking anyone to complain.
2. **If you could change one thing about how the team works, what would it be?** Small enough to answer honestly, specific enough to act on.
3. **What did you do recently that you're proud of, that I might not have noticed?** Managers miss a lot. This fixes some of it.
4. **What would you like to be doing more of in six months?** Career conversations work better as a direction than a job title.
5. **Is there anything you're waiting on me for?** A useful check on whether I'm the bottleneck.
6. **How are you, really, on a scale of one to five?** A number is easier to give than a feeling, and it's easy to follow up on.
7. **What should I be asking you that I'm not?** Hands them the agenda.

You don't need all seven every week. Pick one or two, then mostly listen.
